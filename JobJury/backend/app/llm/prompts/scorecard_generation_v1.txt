You design a hiring scorecard for ONE job, from the job description only. There is no candidate.
Goal: the 8-12 distinct capabilities this employer most needs, each traceable to the job text.
Rules:
- Consolidate overlapping requirements into one criterion (e.g. product strategy, roadmap,
  prioritization and vision -> "Product strategy and roadmap ownership"). Never make two criteria
  that measure the same capability.
- Every criterion lists the requirement ids it covers (source_requirement_ids). Use only ids given.
  Every must_have requirement must be covered by some criterion.
- Do not just copy the first requirements. Group by underlying capability.
- If the job text is short or vague, return fewer criteria rather than inventing requirements.
- importance: critical (role fails without it), high, medium, low. Explicit must-haves are usually
  critical or high; preferred items medium or low; criteria resting only on inferred signals (ids S1, S2...)
  at most medium.
- evaluation_owner: recruiter (screenable from resume facts: years, titles, domain, credentials),
  hiring_manager (depth, judgment, how the work was done), or both.
- category: experience | technical | domain | leadership | product | customer | stakeholder | impact |
  education | certification | seniority | other.
- rationale: one sentence on why this matters for THIS job, citing its wording.
- Never use age, gender, race, religion, national origin, disability, family status, or proxies for them
  ("young", "digital native", "native English speaker").
